Knowledge and Curriculum

Every topic asked in the 6 papers (2018 – 2024), grouped and explained. No official answer key was available, so these are model answers.

Knowledge and Education

Types, forms, sources and comparisons appear in every paper.

Meaning of education; what is knowledge 2+3

Education: the lifelong process of gaining knowledge, skills, values and attitudes, and of developing the whole person.

Knowledge: understanding of facts, ideas and skills that is true, justified and gained through experience, reasoning or learning.

Sources of knowledge 3–5
  • Sense experience: seeing, hearing, touching (empiricism)
  • Reason: logic and thinking (rationalism), e.g. mathematics
  • Authority: teachers, books, experts, tradition
  • Intuition: sudden insight
  • Revelation: religious and spiritual texts
  • Scientific method: observation, experiment, testing
  • Language, media and ICT
Types of knowledge 4–5
  • Propositional: knowing that (facts)
  • Procedural: knowing how (skills)
  • Knowledge by acquaintance: from direct experience
  • A priori (by reason alone) and a posteriori (from experience)
  • Explicit (written) and tacit (personal, hard to express)
Concept and forms of knowledge 4–6

Knowledge is organised, tested understanding, not a heap of facts. Paul Hirst described distinct forms of knowledge, each with its own concepts and tests of truth:

  • Mathematics, physical sciences, human sciences
  • History, religion, literature and fine arts
  • Philosophy and morals
Knowledge vs information, skill, truth and belief 5–8
  • Knowledge vs information: information is raw data or facts; knowledge is information understood, organised and applied. Information can be copied; knowledge is built by the learner.
  • Knowledge vs skill: knowledge is knowing; skill is the ability to do, gained by practice.
  • Belief vs truth: belief is what a person accepts as true and may be wrong; truth matches reality and can be checked.
  • Knowledge vs truth: knowledge needs truth, belief and justification together.
Logical basis of knowledge 4–5
  • Knowledge is built by reasoning: deduction (general to particular) and induction (particular to general)
  • Concepts form principles; principles form theories
  • Claims must be consistent and supported by evidence
  • This is why subjects follow a sequence and every step rests on earlier ones
Epistemological basis of curriculum 2+3

Epistemology is the study of the nature, sources and validity of knowledge.

  • It decides what knowledge is worth teaching
  • School subjects reflect the forms of knowledge
  • Shapes the content, its order and the methods (inquiry, discovery)
  • Curriculum must separate fact from opinion and stay up to date
Structure of discipline and disciplinary knowledge 2+3

Each discipline has its own key concepts, principles, language and methods of inquiry.

  • Distinct concepts and special vocabulary
  • Own methods of finding truth (experiment in science, sources in history)
  • Systematic and cumulative
  • Changes as new knowledge grows

Society, Knowledge and Knowledge Management

Society role and knowledge commission in 5 of 6 papers.

How society generates and disseminates knowledge 5
  • Generates: research institutions, universities, scientists, traditional and local knowledge, industry, innovations
  • Disseminates: family, community, schools, libraries, books, press, radio, TV, internet
  • Society sets needs and priorities and funds research
  • Examples: oral tradition passing folk wisdom; the printing press spreading learning; the internet making knowledge instantly available
Knowledge explosion; sociological basis of education 2+3 / 2½+2½

Knowledge explosion: the very rapid growth of new knowledge and information.

Effects on society

  • Fast change in jobs and technology
  • Information overload; need to judge what is reliable
  • More specialisation; overcrowded curriculum
  • Need for lifelong learning and ICT skills
  • Digital divide between those with and without access

Sociological basis

  • Education transmits culture and prepares learners for social roles
  • It must update curriculum and teach learning-to-learn
  • It must serve national concerns: equality, employment, unity
National Knowledge Commission: recommendations 5

Set up in 2005 to prepare India for a knowledge society.

  • Expand access: Right to Education and more quality schools
  • Expand higher education and improve quality
  • Strengthen libraries and a national network of knowledge
  • Teach English as a language from the early years
  • Promote vocational education, research and open educational resources
  • Use ICT and e-governance to share knowledge
Knowledge management: meaning, strategies, uses 2+3 / 5

Creating, sharing, storing and using knowledge so that an organisation learns and improves.

Strategies at school level

  • Regular teacher sharing sessions and peer mentoring
  • Documenting good practice and lesson plans in a common digital store
  • School library and resource centre
  • Subject committees and professional learning communities
  • Student portfolios; use of the school website and ICT

Uses to a teacher

  • Saves time by reusing good material
  • Learn from colleagues; better teaching
  • Professional growth and consistent quality

Modernization, Democracy and Social Change

Asked in all 6 papers.

Education as a tool of modernization and democratic citizenship 5

Modernization

  • Spreads scientific temper and rational thinking
  • Gives skills for industry and technology
  • Reduces superstition and blind customs
  • Encourages mobility, new ideas and innovation

Democratic citizenship

  • Teaches rights, duties and the Constitution
  • Develops critical thinking and respect for others
  • Builds equality, liberty, fraternity and justice
  • Trains for participation: voting, school councils, discussion
Modern values related to education 1+4 / 5
  • Equality and social justice; equity
  • Secularism and respect for diversity
  • Scientific temper and rational thinking
  • Democracy and human rights; gender equality
  • Environmental concern; national integration; global citizenship

These are taught through the curriculum, textbooks and school activities.

Equity, equality and social justice through education 10
  • Equality: same chance for all, such as the Right to Education and free textbooks
  • Equity: extra support to those who need it: scholarships, reservations, inclusive education, mid-day meal
  • Social justice: removing caste, gender and class bias through curriculum and school practice
Role of education in social change 5
  • Spreads awareness and new ideas
  • Reduces caste, dowry, child marriage and other evils
  • Raises the status of women and weaker sections
  • Gives occupational mobility and reduces poverty
  • Builds scientific outlook and leadership for reform

Curriculum and Its Types

Meaning, characteristics and types in all papers.

Meaning and characteristics of curriculum 2+3

Curriculum is the whole plan of learning experiences a school offers: aims, content, methods, activities and evaluation.

  • Purposeful: linked to aims of education
  • Child-related and flexible
  • Broad: includes co-curricular activities
  • Relevant to society and environment
  • Balanced and integrated
  • Dynamic: revised with change
  • Can be evaluated
Learner centered curriculum 4–5
  • Built around learner needs, interests and abilities
  • Active learning through projects and activities
  • Choice and flexibility; respects individual differences
  • Teacher acts as facilitator
  • Continuous and varied assessment
Community centered and environment centered curriculum 4–5
  • Uses the local community and surroundings as learning resources
  • Field trips, surveys, service projects, local crafts and problems
  • Builds environmental awareness and responsibility
  • Connects school with life; involves community in school
Humanistic curriculum 2+3 / 5

Focuses on the growth of the whole person, feelings and values (Maslow, Rogers).

  • Self-esteem, freedom and responsibility
  • Learning in a supportive, non-threatening climate
  • Emphasis on values, creativity and cooperation
  • Teacher as caring facilitator
  • Aims at self-actualization: reaching ones full potential
Social reconstructivist curriculum and teacher role 2+3 / 5

Uses education to improve and reform society (Brameld, Dewey, Freire). Content is drawn from real social problems: poverty, inequality, environment, injustice.

  • Critical thinking, discussion and action projects
  • Community involvement; democratic classroom
  • Relevant today because students face social and environmental issues

Role of teacher

  • Agent of change who raises awareness of social problems
  • Guides inquiry and projects; encourages questions
  • Models fairness and democratic values

Frameworks, Textbooks and National Goals

NCFTE 2009 and national goals in all papers.

Curriculum framework; curriculum, syllabus and textbook 2+3 / 5+5

Curriculum framework: national guideline of vision, aims and principles from which curriculum, syllabi and textbooks are prepared (NCERT). It brings unity and quality and is revised periodically.

  • Curriculum: broad; aims, content, methods, activities, evaluation
  • Syllabus: narrow; subject-wise list of topics and units with time
  • Textbook: one resource used to transact the syllabus; a supplement to curriculum, not the whole curriculum
Salient features of NCF 2005 5
  • Connect knowledge to life outside school
  • Shift from rote learning
  • Go beyond the textbook
  • Flexible examinations integrated with classroom life
  • Nurture a caring identity within a democratic polity
  • Constructivist, child centered approach; inclusion and learning without burden
  • Peace, gender, environment and values across subjects
Salient features of NCFTE 2009 (teacher education) 5
  • Teacher as reflective practitioner and agent of change
  • Constructivist, learner centered pedagogy
  • Longer programmes (2-year B.Ed, 4-year integrated)
  • Strong school internship; link between theory and practice
  • Inclusion, gender and values; use of ICT
  • Professional development of teacher educators
Textbooks and digital learning material in curriculum transaction 5
  • Textbooks: organised content, common standard, support revision and self-study
  • Digital material: interactive, multimedia, simulations, e-books and open resources; self-paced; reaches remote learners
  • Teacher should use them with activities and discussion, not replace teaching
School curriculum and national goals; state vs central syllabus 5
  • National goals: equality, national integration, scientific temper, values, employability
  • Curriculum helps through constitutional values, inclusive content, activity based learning, vocational and ICT skills
  • Central (NCERT/CBSE): uniform, national framework
  • State: follows NCF but adds regional language, culture and context
  • To analyse: check alignment with NCF 2005 and 2009, activity based methods, inclusion, and local relevance

Multiculturalism and Multilingual Education

Asked in all 6 papers.

Multicultural education: meaning, uses 1+4

Education that respects and includes different cultures, languages, religions and castes.

  • Builds national integration and tolerance
  • Reduces prejudice and conflict
  • Includes every child; improves achievement
  • Prepares students for a diverse society
Need for critical multiculturalism; value concerns and gender 4+3+3

Critical multiculturalism goes beyond celebrating festivals. It questions power and inequality (caste, class, gender, religion).

  • India has deep diversity and inequality
  • Develops critical thinking and fairness
  • Value concerns: respect, justice, equality
  • Gender: remove bias in textbooks, classroom roles and opportunities
Strategies for multicultural and multilingual education 6
  • Use the home language as a bridge; bilingual teaching
  • Include examples, stories and heroes from many cultures
  • Celebrate all festivals; cultural programmes
  • Mixed cooperative groups; local resource persons
  • Bias-free materials and fair classroom rules
  • Three-language formula and mother tongue in early years
Social sensitivity and economic diversity in school programme 2½+2½
  • Children come from very different economic backgrounds
  • Gaps in resources can cause exclusion, shame and unequal results
  • Schools need empathy, equal treatment, scholarships, shared resources and mixed groups
